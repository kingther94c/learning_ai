What the model would sample from

Bars are probabilities after your settings. Greyed-out tokens have been cut by top-k or top-p and can never be sampled, no matter how the temperature is set.

p = softmax(logits / T)

Temperature divides the logits before the softmax. Dividing by a number below 1 stretches the gaps apart (sharper); above 1 squashes them together (flatter). It cannot reorder anything — the ranking is identical at every temperature.

The failure modes: at low temperature a model loops ("the the the"), because the top token stays top forever. At high temperature it wanders into tokens it assigned almost no mass to. top-k and top-p exist to cut that tail off so you can raise temperature without inviting nonsense.

Why top-p usually beats top-k

The number of tokens each method keeps, swept across every context in this dataset. top-k keeps a constant count by definition. top-p adapts: after a predictable context it keeps 1–2 tokens; after an open-ended one it keeps many.

That adaptivity is the whole argument. A fixed k = 10 is far too generous when the model is certain — it keeps nine tokens that together hold under 1% of the mass, and sampling promotes one of them roughly 1% of the time. The same k = 10 is too stingy when the model is genuinely uncertain across thirty plausible options.

top-p asks a better question: how many tokens do I need before I have covered p of the probability mass? The answer is small when the model is confident and large when it is not, which is exactly the behaviour you want.
